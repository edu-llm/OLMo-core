## FINDING 1 — REFUTED: the GDN2 "significant" CI [+0.0262, +0.0479] cannot be reproduced by any procedure, and the correct Dunnett-T3 CI **includes zero**

This is the most serious finding, because it is the one contested claim that could move a
production decision toward or away from GDN2.

I ran the repo's own `welch_t3_contrasts()` on the full 18 cells. Its output for GDN2:

```
GDN2  est=+0.037055  se=0.006631  welch_df=2.5548  crit=5.7087
      CI=[-0.00080, +0.07491]   excludes_zero=False
```

The script itself says **not significant**. The claim in `HANDOFF.md:53-56` and in
`RESULTS-TABLE.html:165,245` is `[+0.0262, +0.0479]`, excluding zero. Those are different
intervals and only one of them came out of the code.

Why the claimed interval is not merely different but *structurally impossible*:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| claimed half-width | 0.010850 |
| Welch SE (n=3 vs n=3) | 0.006631 |
| **implied critical value** | **1.6362** |
| z(.975) | 1.9600 |
| t(.975, df=2.5548) — *uncorrected, single comparison* | 3.5203 |
| SMM k=5, df=2.5548 — the correct T3 critical value | 5.7087 |

A critical value of 1.64 is below even the large-sample uncorrected 1.96. A CI that claims to be
**corrected for k=5 comparisons at df=2.55** cannot be narrower than an uncorrected large-sample
interval — the correction and the small df both only widen it. The claimed interval is ~3.5x too
narrow. (Best-matching arithmetic accident: SMM(k=5, df=3.4054) × GDN2's *own* SEM 0.002335 =
0.010764, i.e. a critical value from one arm's df paired with a standard error that omits the
control's variance entirely. That is a guess at provenance, not a reconstruction — label it
**SUSPECTED**.)

**The T3 code is not the bug.** I MC-validated the script's SMM at every df its T3 path uses:

| df | script SMM k=5 | MC (6M draws) |
|---|---|---|
| 2.2260 | 6.5026 | 6.4994 |
| 2.5548 | 5.7088 | 5.7137 |
| 3.2667 | 4.7358 | 4.7362 |
| 3.4054 | 4.6108 | 4.6085 |

It also reduces exactly to Student t at k=1 (diff ≤ 5.6e-11) and sits just below its Bonferroni
bound at every df. The T3 implementation is correct; **the number in the prose is not from the
code.**

**Under the correct T3, no arm's CI excludes zero:**

### 1c — CONFIRMED and serious: the T3 branch was **never licensed to run at all**

Pre-registration §4.5 is unambiguous: *"Levene (median-centred, robust) as the decision test,
Bartlett reported alongside. **If Levene rejects at α = 0.05**, fall back to Welch's ANOVA with
Games-Howell."* Levene p = 0.4840. It does not reject. **The fallback is not engaged**, and the
script correctly leaves `welch_fallback: null`.

So the sentence "Under the **pre-registered** unequal-variance fallback … GDN2's Dunnett-T3 CI is

triggered by the pre-registered rule, the *procedure* substitution (T3 for Games-Howell) is a

*"Committing to this now is what stops the fallback being chosen after seeing which answer it
gives."* The rule was written correctly and then not followed.

**What IS pre-registered, verified line by line:** the Levene-decides/Bartlett-reports rule
(§4.5), the Games-Howell fallback (§4.5 — T3 is the declared substitution), Dunnett k=5 df=12
crit 2.902 (§4.4), exact non-central t dominant-tail power (§5), and — verbatim at §5, line

an expected outcome."* **The "CE may not resolve" claim is genuinely pre-registered. The
"GDN2 is significant under the pre-registered fallback" claim is not.**

## FINDING 2 — CONFIRMED, and it changes nothing: the design *is* seed-paired, the analysis *is* unpaired, and exploiting the pairing makes the answer very slightly **worse**

The criticism is legitimate and deserves the explicit number you asked for. Here it is.

Randomized-block (two-way, no interaction), data seed as block:

| source | SS | df | MS |
|---|---|---|---|
| arm | 5.187374e-03 | 5 | 1.0375e-03 |
| **block (data seed)** | **9.070656e-04** | **2** | 4.5353e-04 |
| residual | 4.094259e-03 | 10 | 4.0943e-04 |
| total | 1.018870e-02 | 17 | |

(SS decomposition closes exactly; residual 0.00e+00.)

| | unpaired (as analysed) | **blocked (paired)** |
|---|---|---|
| σ̂ | 0.020415, df 12 | **0.020234, df 10** |
| χ² interval | [0.01464, 0.03370] | [0.01414, 0.03551] |
| F(arm) | F(5,12) = 2.4893, p = 0.0908 | F(5,10) = 2.5340, p = 0.0989 |
| Dunnett crit k=5 | 2.90126 | **2.99029** |
| SE(diff) | 0.016669 | 0.016521 |
| **MDE (exact non-central t, dominant tail, 80%)** | **0.06360** | **0.06481** |

**Blocking makes the MDE 1.9% worse.** The reason is decisive:

- **F_block(2,10) = 1.1077, p = 0.3677.** The block effect is not detectable.
- Variance component of the data-seed block = 7.35e-06; **ICC = 0.0176**. The shared data order
  explains **1.8%** of within-arm variance.
- Removing it costs 2 residual df, and at these df the Dunnett critical value rises from 2.901
  to 2.990. The 0.9% reduction in σ̂ does not pay for the df.

Blocked Dunnett contrasts (exact equicorrelated max-|t|, ρ=½, df=10): every CI still includes
zero, every adjusted p is within 0.006 of the unpaired one, and GDN2 is +0.03705 with CI
[−0.01235, +0.08646], adj p = 0.1639 (unpaired: 0.1585). **No conclusion changes.**

The honest framing for the lead: *we designed it paired, analysed it unpaired, and I checked —
the pairing was worth 1.8% of the variance, so the unpaired analysis is not merely defensible, it
is the slightly more powerful one.* The pre-registration is in fact internally consistent here:
§2.2 states plainly that init seeds cannot be paired across arms and *"this document does not
claim any"* variance reduction from seed reuse, and §5 sizes the MDE on the **"unpaired difference
of two arm means (SE = σ√(2/3))"**. The unpaired analysis is what was pre-registered. The
pairing exists for the run-1↔run-2 drift check (§7), which is its real job.

**The deeper implication, and it is the one that matters for your money decision: 98.2% of the
within-arm variance is NOT data order.** It is init seed plus kernel nondeterminism. No amount of
cleverness with the data-seed block will reduce it — only more replicates will.

Per-seed paired differences vs control (uncorrected paired t, df=2 — **not** a licensed test,
shown for completeness): GDN2 +0.04837/+0.02060/+0.04219, mean +0.03705, **t(2) = 4.40, p =
0.0479**. All three seeds agree in sign for GDN2 and for KDA_R2. That directional consistency is
real information, but at df=2 and uncorrected it is not a significance claim.

On block-residuals, as you asked:

| basis | Levene W | Levene p | Bartlett χ² | Bartlett p | sd ratio max/min |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw | 0.9506 | 0.4840 | 16.3667 | 0.0059 | 17.50x |
| block-centred (y − block mean) | 0.9502 | 0.4843 | 12.4554 | **0.0291** | **17.79x** |
| full two-way residuals | 0.9502 | 0.4843 | 12.4554 | 0.0291 | 17.79x |

**The verdict does not flip, and your hypothesis is REFUTED in its specifics.** Removing the seed
block does not shrink KDA_R1's spread — it *slightly widens* the ratio (17.50x → 17.79x). KDA_R1's
block-centred values are [+0.00172, **+0.05743**, −0.01329]: the 3.12675 cell is +0.0574 *above*

that this call could not be made after seeing which answer it gave.** Following the rule is
correct here. Reporting Bartlett alongside is also correct. Using Bartlett's rejection to justify
switching to the branch that produces a significant GDN2 is the thing the rule forbids.

## FINDING 5 — the MDE gate is stricter than α=0.05, which makes "not resolved" *conservatively* true

`build_recommendation()` requires **CI excludes zero AND |estimate| > MDE**. These are not the
same threshold:

- Dunnett CI threshold on |est| = crit × SE = **0.04836**
- MDE threshold on |est| = **0.06360** (1.315x larger — **the MDE binds**)
- implied |t| threshold = **3.8157**, not 2.90126
- **familywise size of that conjunction = 0.00991**, against a pre-registered α of 0.05

**This is a real methodological confusion**: an MDE is a *design* quantity (what size of effect
this n could have detected) and it is being used as an *inference* threshold (how big an observed
effect must be to count). Requiring an observed effect to exceed the MDE is a well-known error —
it makes the test roughly 5x more conservative than declared. It is nowhere in the
pre-registration, which §4.6 says only "effect, Dunnett-adjusted CI and n for every contrast".

**It does not change the headline** — GDN2's |t| = 2.2230 fails even the correct 2.90126 gate, so
"not resolved" survives the correction. But it must be flagged, because if a future run lands an
arm between the two thresholds, the code will call it unresolved when the pre-registered α=0.05
Dunnett rule calls it resolved. **UNSUPPORTED as a pre-registered rule; a live trap for run 2.**

## FINDING 6 — multiplicity and forking paths: blunt answer

**How many analysis branches exist?** {pooled one-way, Welch/T3, blocked} × {Levene decides,
Bartlett decides} × {all 6 arms, drop KDA_R1} × {5 control contrasts, 15 all-pairs} × {corrected,
uncorrected} × {paired, unpaired}. Well over a hundred reachable numbers.

**What reaches p < 0.05 in each family:**

| family | correction | GDN2 significant? |
|---|---|---|
| Dunnett vs control, pooled (**the pre-registered primary**) | Dunnett k=5 | **no**, adj p = 0.1585 |
| Blocked Dunnett vs control | Dunnett k=5, df=10 | **no**, adj p = 0.1639 |
| Dunnett-T3, correctly computed (**unlicensed branch**) | SMM k=5 | **no**, CI [−0.0008, +0.0749] |
| Tukey HSD, all 15 pairs | studentized range | **no** — nothing < 0.05; best is NOACT-vs-GDN2 p = 0.1058 |
| pooled t, uncorrected | none | yes, p = 0.0462 (and 3 of 15 pairs < 0.05) |
| Welch t, uncorrected | none | yes, p = 0.0171 |
| paired t(2), uncorrected | none | yes, p = 0.0479 |
| Dunnett with KDA_R1 dropped | Dunnett k=4, df=10 | yes, adj p = 0.0027 |

**Every corrected route says no. Every route that says yes is either uncorrected or drops an arm
with no pre-registered exclusion rule.** Note the last row especially: dropping KDA_R1 cuts σ̂
from 0.02042 to 0.00961 and makes GDN2 adj p = 0.0027 with CI [+0.01439, +0.05972]. That is the
analysis that would produce a headline, and it is exactly the analysis the pre-registration's
"never exclude-then-cite" and no-outlier-rule provisions forbid. The script is right to refuse it;
I show it only as sensitivity.

**Verdict: reporting "GDN2 significant under the fallback" is a garden-of-forking-paths artifact,
and a compound one** — it needed (a) Bartlett to override the designated decision test, (b) an
unlicensed branch, and (c) a CI that no procedure produces. The generated `REPORT.md` is clean and
commits none of this. **The contamination is entirely in the human-written layer** — `HANDOFF.md`
and `RESULTS-TABLE.html`/`.pdf` — which are the artifacts a lead and Tom Liu will actually read.
`HANDOFF.md` even calls the run "a **well-powered** NULL"; it is the opposite — MDE 0.0636 against
a 0.010–0.030 target is a badly underpowered null, correctly reported as a bound. Also note the
PDF's "KDA_R1 … dissolved GDN2's otherwise-significant contrast" — that frames a pre-registered
pooled analysis as damage rather than as the result.

"this design cannot distinguish the gate from no-gate, and cannot rule out an effect of either
sign up to 0.04 nats." The pre-registration's own language is better than the HANDOFF's.

## FINDING 8 — is pooling defensible at 17.5x sd ratio? Yes, but say what it costs

Per-arm sd: KDA_NOACT 0.00258 · KDA_BASE 0.01075 · GDN2 0.00404 · KDA_R2 0.00642 ·
KDA_GCONV 0.01678 · **KDA_R1 0.04516**. Ratio 17.50x on sd, **306x on variance**.

The single 3.12675 cell (KDA_R1, seed 220014) carries this entirely:

- it is **8.25 σ** above its own arm's other two cells (σ from a leave-one-out pool)
- KDA_R1 alone contributes **81.6%** of the total SS_within
- σ̂ with the cell: **0.02042**; without it: **0.00942** — **one cell of 18 inflates σ̂ 2.17x**
- dropping KDA_R1 entirely: σ̂ = 0.00961, F(5,10) = 11.776, p = 0.0008

**Pooling is nonetheless the right call, for three reasons.** (1) It is what was pre-registered,
and the alternative was chosen after seeing which answer it gives. (2) There is no pre-registered
outlier rule, and inventing one now is the failure a pre-registration exists to prevent. (3) The
unequal-variance alternative is *worse*, not better, at n=3: Welch df collapses to 2.23–3.41
(structurally capped at 4 for n=3 vs n=3), which drives the SMM critical value to 4.6–6.5 and
makes KDA_R1's own CI ±0.17 nats — 4x wider than the pooled one. **Heteroscedastic methods do not
rescue you at n=3; they cost more df than the variance heterogeneity costs.**

**What I would have done differently, concretely:**

1. **Reported both σ̂ figures with the mechanism named**: "σ̂ = 0.02042 (all six arms) but 0.00961
   excluding KDA_R1, whose spread is a *suspected kernel artifact on mathematically identical
   computation*." The pre-registration's own §6 says run 2 must be sized from this number — and
   sizing run 2 from 0.02042 when 0.00961 is the σ of every arm you would actually ship is an
   expensive mistake in the conservative direction. The HANDOFF's "size any future run from
   0.02042" is over-conservative by a factor of ~4.5x in n.
2. **Pre-registered a robustness protocol, not an exclusion rule** — report the primary pooled
   analysis and a declared leave-one-arm-out sensitivity, so the reader sees the fragility instead
   of the analyst choosing.
3. **Pre-registered a variance-stabilising diagnostic on the co-primary** — KDA_R1's sd being 17x
   its FLOP-identical twin is itself a finding about numerical stability, and belongs in the
   report as a *result*, not as a nuisance that "damaged the run's power".
4. **Never used the MDE as an inference threshold** (Finding 5).

**Also flagged: the outlier guard cannot fire.** `OUTLIER_WARN_NATS = 0.5` against σ̂ = 0.0204 is
a **24.5 σ** threshold. The largest actual deviation is 3.60 σ (0.0734 nats) — the R1 cell — and
the guard stayed silent on it. A guard calibrated 7x beyond the admissibility band's own
resolution is unreachable on any run that converged. It should be in σ units (e.g. > 4 σ from the
arm's own median), not nats.

## FINDING 10 — the run did not execute the pre-registered design (context for every number above)

Not my primary scope, but it conditions the statistics and the report handles it honestly, so it
belongs here. `seeds.json` froze **1,907 steps / 999,817,216 tokens / TPP 2.56**. All 18 cells ran
**1,144 steps / 599,785,472 tokens / TPP 1.5** — a 40% budget cut. The script deliberately holds
admissibility to the *modal realised* step count rather than the frozen plan (all 18 agree on
1,144, so pairing is intact), declares the deviation, and states correctly that the cut makes CE
magnitudes **more** inflated. That is the right handling. But note the pre-registration's
§5.1/§7 language that `--steps` is "load-bearing for the pairing, not just for cost" and that a
run trimming steps "consumes a prefix of the stream" — **run 2 must use 1,144, not the frozen
1,907, or the repeated-baseline drift check is void.** That is not written down anywhere yet.

I also note several cells carry visibly rounded telemetry (`last_loss: 3.0` exactly on 6 cells,
`first_loss: 11.7124` on 16, `seconds: 1846.0`) while `val_ce` is full float64 precision
throughout. `val_ce` — the only field my analysis uses — is unaffected, and A1/E1 are better
placed to chase provenance. **Flagged, not adjudicated.**

| 0.020 | 24 | 144 | $6,324 | +126 cells, ~$5,533 |
| 0.010 (literature lower end) | **95** | 570 | **$25,000** | +552 cells |

Sensitivity to the σ̂ bracket is brutal — at 0.030 nats, n ranges **6 → 11 → 30** across the χ²
interval [0.01464, 0.03370]. And **if KDA_R1 is excluded (σ = 0.00961), resolving 0.030 nats needs
n = 3 — you already have it.**

**The cheap decisive option nobody costed: a 2-arm run.** KDA_BASE vs GDN2 only, k=1 so Dunnett
reduces to plain t:

| target effect | n/arm | cells | ~cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.037 | 6 | 12 | **$527** |
| 0.030 | 9 | 18 | $790 |
| 0.020 | 18 | 36 | $1,581 |

The bake-off's k=5 correction is costing you most of your power on four contrasts you no longer

apply — including the two that were argued to overturn it.** The pooled Dunnett analysis is
