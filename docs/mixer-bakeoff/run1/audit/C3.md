## 2. Is the gate genuinely per-channel `[B,T,H,K]`? — **CONFIRMED (conclusion), but the handoff's stated *reason* is wrong)**

Per-channel is the **native and only** supported shape — not something merely tolerated:

- `chunk_rwkv6_fwd_cumsum` unpacks `B, T, H, S = g.shape` (`fla/ops/rwkv6/chunk.py:96`).
  A 3-D `[B,T,H]` gate raises a **ValueError on unpack**, immediately.
- gi/ge are indexed as genuine 2-D `(T,K)` blocks with stride `(H*K, 1)`
  (`dplr/chunk_A_fwd.py:82-83`, `:208-209`), and `exp2` is applied elementwise per channel
  (`chunk_A_fwd.py:100-105`).
- Backward reads it per-channel too: `gk + ((bos+last_idx)*H + i_h)*K + tl.arange(0,BK)`
  (`dplr/chunk_h_bwd.py:104`).
- Contrast: `chunk_gated_delta_product` **does** assert `g.shape == (B,T,H)`
  (`gated_delta_product/chunk.py:344`) — so the handoff's premise about *that* op is right.

**Correction to the handoff:** "no shape assert" is literally true (I grepped every assert in
`dplr/` — only `BK<=256`, `NK==1`, and the CP asserts exist) but it is a *misleading* framing.
A wrong shape does **not** silently broadcast or corrupt; it raises in the cumsum unpack. The
honest statement is stronger than the handoff's: **DPLR requires a per-channel gate.**

## 4. Is the mathematical mapping correct? — **REFUTED as literally written (off by one Hadamard factor); the *shared-decay argument* is CONFIRMED**

**DPLR's recurrence, from source.** `dplr/fused_recurrent.py:93-94`:

```
b_h = exp(b_gk)[:, None] * b_h + b_b[:, None] * tl.sum(b_a[:, None] * b_h, 0)[None, :]
b_h += b_k[:, None] * b_v[None, :]
```

Both RHS references are to the *old* `b_h`, so with state `S ∈ R^{K×V}`:

> **S_t = Diag(α_t)·S_{t-1} + b_t·(a_tᵀ S_{t-1}) + k_t v_tᵀ**,  α_t = exp(g_t)

Confirmed independently by the naive reference (`dplr/naive.py:34-35`, where naive's `alpha`/`beta`
are the `a`/`b` vectors) and by the chunkwise form, which scales `a` by the **exclusive** cumsum
and `q` by the **inclusive** one (`naive.py:85` vs `:95`; kernel: `b_ag = b_a*exp2(b_ge)`,
`b_qg = b_q*exp2(b_gi)`, `chunk_A_fwd.py:100-105`; `gi`=inclusive/`ge`=exclusive per
`rwkv6/chunk.py:71-80`).

**The critical structural fact: the low-rank term reads the state BEFORE that step's decay.**

**Our target**, from our own kernel (`.../src/olmo_core/nn/attention/kda_householder.py:17-24`,
and the Triton body at `:185-197`):

```
S⁽⁰⁾ = Diag(α_t)·S_{t-1}                                    # decay once per token
S⁽ʳ⁺¹⁾ = (I − β_r k_r k_rᵀ)·S⁽ʳ⁾ + β_r k_r v_rᵀ              # r = 0..R−1
o_t = q_tᵀ S⁽ᴿ⁾
```

(`b_u = b_beta*(b_v - sum(b_h*b_k))`, `b_h += b_k⊗b_u` at `:196-197` is exactly
`S ← (I − β k kᵀ)S + β k vᵀ`. With `‖k‖₂=1` — enforced at
`.../recurrent.py:1669-1670` — and `β=2`, `(I − 2kkᵀ)` is an exact reflection, eigenvalue −1
along `k`. The negative-eigenvalue mechanism is real.)

**Substituting the handoff's mapping at r = 0** (decay on, `a_0 = k_0`, `b_0 = −β_0 k_0`):

```
DPLR gives:  Diag(α)S_{t-1} − β_0 k_0 (k_0ᵀ S_{t-1})       + βk_0 v_0ᵀ
Target is:   Diag(α)S_{t-1} − β_0 k_0 (k_0ᵀ Diag(α)S_{t-1}) + βk_0 v_0ᵀ
                                            ^^^^^^^^^
```

These are **not equal**. The reflection must read the *decayed* state; DPLR's `a`-term reads the
*undecayed* one. **`a_r = k_r` is wrong at r = 0.**

**The fix, and why the shared-decay argument nevertheless holds.** Set

> **a_0 = α_t ⊙ k_0**  (elementwise over K; α_t = exp(g_t)),  a_r = k_r for r ≥ 1

Then `a_0ᵀ S_{t-1} = k_0ᵀ Diag(α_t) S_{t-1}` exactly. For r ≥ 1, `gk = 0` ⇒ `Diag(α)=I` and
`a_r = k_r`, `b_r = −β_r k_r` reproduces `(I − β_r k_r k_rᵀ)S + β_r k_r v_rᵀ` **exactly**, with no
correction.

So the handoff's *conceptual* claim — **"the diagonal is absorbed into k rather than commuted"** —
is **CORRECT**, and it does **not** require `Diag(α)` to commute with the reflections: there is one
decay, applied once, before any reflection, and it is absorbed into the `a` vector of factor 0 only.
This works precisely because DPLR keeps `a` and `k` as *separate arguments*, so `a` can be
pre-scaled without touching `k`. **The math does survive R>1 exactly.** But the *written mapping*
`a_r = k_r` omits the `α ⊙` factor and would produce a subtly wrong operator that trains happily
(α = exp(g) ≤ 1 and is often near 1, so the error is small and silent). This is the single most
important correction in this audit.

`q` on the last factor: **CONFIRMED correct.** `o` at interleaved position `(t, R−1)` is
`q_tᵀ S⁽ᴿ⁾`, which is the target readout; outputs at `r < R−1` are computed and discarded.
`b_r = −β_r k_r` also means `dk` accumulates from three DPLR arguments (`k'`, `a`, `b`) and `dβ`
from two — but since you build them from `k`/`β` with ordinary torch ops, **autograd does this for
you. The handoff's "no gradients hand-derived" is CONFIRMED and is the proposal's real strength.**

## 6. Is the named conditioning risk real? — **REFUTED for the *named* risk; a DIFFERENT real risk found**

The handoff names `gk = 0` and `q = 0` on R−1 of every R positions as "the riskiest unknown."
Examining the backward:

- **There are no divisions at all in `dplr/`.** I grepped `tl.fdiv`, `1.0/`, `1/`, `rcp`,
  `reciprocal`, `rsqrt`, `/ b_`, `/ (` across all 11 `dplr/*.py`: the only hits are the docstrings
  `"1 / sqrt(K)"` (`chunk.py:476`, `fused_recurrent.py:232`). **No `1/(1−x)`, no matrix inverse of
  a possibly-singular matrix, no reciprocal of the gate.**
- **`gk = 0` is numerically FAVOURABLE, not hazardous.** The one genuinely dangerous expression is
  `inv_exp_gi = exp2(−b_gi_val)` (`chunk_A_fwd.py:237`, and `g_exp_inv = exp2(−b_gi + b_g_last)` at
  `:101`) — an *inverse* exponential of accumulated log-decay, which blows up when `gi` is very
  negative. Interleaving with `gk=0` on R−1 of R positions makes the within-chunk cumsum **less**
  negative (a BT=16 chunk covers only 8 real tokens at R=2), so `exp2(−gi)` gets **smaller and
  safer**. The safe-gate path additionally re-centres on a mid-chunk offset
  (`chunk_A_fwd.py:225-232`) specifically to bound this.
- **`q = 0` is inert.** `q` enters only multiplicatively (`b_qg = b_q*g_exp`, `chunk_A_fwd.py:102`)
  and through `A_qk`/`A_qb` dot products; zero `q` ⇒ zero rows, zero `dq` contributions. Nothing
  divides by `q`.

**The real conditioning risk is elsewhere: the `A_ab` triangular inverse.**
`prepare_wy_repr_fwd` (`wy_fast_fwd.py:252-289`, kernels at `:30-59`/`:76-139`) inverts
`I + strictly_lower(A_ab)` by forward substitution. Under the mapping,
`A_ab[i,j] = a_i·b_j·decay = −β_j (k_i·k_j)·decay`. **Because `gk = 0` between the two factors of
the same token, the intra-token entry carries no decay damping** and approaches `−β_0 (k_1·k_0)`,
i.e. magnitude up to ≈2 when the two Householder directions are near-parallel and `β→2`.
The matrix is unit-lower-triangular so it is **always invertible** (det = 1) — no singularity —
but its *conditioning* degrades exactly in the regime the mechanism wants (large β, correlated
k's), and the inverse is computed in the `A_ab` fp32 buffer then **downcast to bf16**
(`wy_fast_fwd.py:135-137`, `:195`) before use. **This, not `gk=0`/`q=0`, is what a day-one probe
should measure.** The handoff named the wrong unknown.

Also unmentioned but required: the backward returns `dgk` for the full interleaved
`[B,T*R,H,K]`, so the wrapper must slice it to `r=0` — exactly as
`gated_delta_product/chunk.py:235` does for its own gate.

## 7. Memory arithmetic — **half CONFIRMED, half REFUTED**

**The 1.00 GiB figure: CONFIRMED, exactly.**
`B·T·H·K·V·4 = 1·4096·16·64·64·4 = 1,073,741,824 B = 1.000 GiB` — matches the fp32 `hs` workspace
allocated at `.../kda_householder.py:539` and warned about at `:534-538`.

**The 32 MiB figure: REFUTED for the R=2 configuration stated.**
The chunked replacement is `h = kg.new_empty(B, NT, H, K, V)` in **bf16** (`chunk_h_fwd.py:154`;
bf16 because `kg = torch.empty_like(k, dtype=q.dtype)`, `chunk_A_fwd.py:340`), with
`NT = T_dplr / BT` and `T_dplr = T·R`. At fla's **effective BT = 16**:

| config | T_dplr | NT | `h` (bf16) |
|---|---|---|---|
| R=1, BT=16 | 4096 | 256 | **32 MiB** ← the quoted number |
| **R=2, BT=16 (the actual case)** | 8192 | 512 | **64 MiB** |
| R=2, BT=64 | 8192 | 128 | 16 MiB |

So "32 MiB" is the **R=1** value, or R=2 at BT=32. **For R=2 at fla's default chunk size it is
64 MiB**, and `h` is not the only live tensor — at R=2 the other DPLR intermediates add
`qg+kg+ag+bg` 64 MiB, `v_new` 16 MiB, `A_ab/A_ak` fp32 16 MiB, `A_qk/A_qb` 8 MiB, `A_ab_inv`
8 MiB ⇒ **≈176 MiB total, not 32 MiB.**

**The memory *case* nevertheless survives:** ~176 MiB vs 1.00 GiB is still ≈6×, and `h` alone is
17×, and — critically — the default `disable_recompute=False` path saves only
`(q,k,v,a,b,gk,initial_state)` and **recomputes** the forward in the backward
(`chunk.py:222`, `:277-323`), which is what bounds it. So: **direction and order of magnitude
CONFIRMED; the specific "1.00 GiB → 32 MiB" is a 2×-optimistic mis-statement.**

*Side finding:* our own code comment is wrong in the same direction —
`.../kda_householder.py:536-537` says "At B4/T4096/H16/K64/V64 it is 6.0 GiB; at
B8/T4096/H16/K128/V128 it is 48 GiB". Correct values are **4.0 GiB** and **32 GiB** (both
overstated by exactly 1.5×). Worth fixing, since these numbers are what size the decision.

`float64`, `gradcheck`-validated torch reference

**What the estimate omits:**

1. **No throughput estimate at all — and throughput is the whole point.** The op runs at
   `T_dplr = T·R`, so at R=2 the four A-matrices (`A_qk, A_qb, A_ak, A_ab` — DPLR builds *four*
   vs GDN's one), the triangular inverse, and the `o` matmul all run at **2× rows**, at

   `disable_recompute=False` forward recomputation in the backward. It is entirely possible this
   lands **slower than the 0.705× it is meant to fix.** Chunking bounds *memory*; it does not
   automatically buy *speed* here.
2. **The `a_0 = α ⊙ k_0` correction** (§4) — one line, but if missed it is silently wrong.
3. **bf16 state vs our fp32 state.** DPLR's `h` and `A_ab_inv` are bf16
   (`chunk_h_fwd.py:154`, `wy_fast_fwd.py:135-137`); our current kernel accumulates the state in
   fp32. Given HANDOFF ⓷ already suspects *kernel-induced instability* at R=1
   (sd 0.04516, 17× the best arm), moving to a lower-precision chunked state is a **variance
   risk**, not an obvious improvement.
4. `dgk` slicing to r=0 (§6); `cu_seqlens * R` for varlen (our layer passes `cu_doc_lens`,

5. **No fp64 path** in fla's chunked kernel, so the wrapper cannot be `gradcheck`ed directly — it

## Bottom line

**Yes, an engineer should start — but on a half-day *measurement*, not on the wrapper.** The
proposal's foundations hold up under source inspection: `chunk_dplr_delta_rule` exists at exactly
the cited path in fla-core 0.5.1, its gate is genuinely (indeed *natively*) per-channel
`[B,T,H,K]`, it is genuinely chunked at BT=16, it is genuinely the production RWKV-7 backend,
`β ∈ (0,2)` violates no assert or clamp in the kernel, autograd really does supply the gradients,
`chunk_gated_delta_product` really is un-patchable (8 files/~20 sites — *worse* than the claimed
six/four, and its backward is shared with production GDN), and — importantly — the
"shared decay is absorbed into k, not commuted" argument is **mathematically correct**: one decay
applied once before any reflection folds into factor 0's `a` vector, with no commutation required.
Three things are wrong and one is inverted. (a) The written mapping `a_r = k_r` is **incorrect at
r = 0**; it must be `a_0 = exp(g_t) ⊙ k_0`, or the operator is subtly wrong *and trains happily*.
(b) "1.00 GiB → 32 MiB" is the R=1 number; at R=2/BT=16 it is 64 MiB for `h` and ≈176 MiB with the
other live intermediates — still a real 6-17× win, so the memory case survives, but do not quote
32 MiB. (c) The named "riskiest unknown" is backwards: `gk=0`/`q=0` are **safe** — there are *zero*
divisions anywhere in `dplr/`, and zeroed gates make the dangerous `exp2(−gi)` term *smaller*.
The actual risk is the `A_ab` unit-triangular inverse, whose intra-token entry approaches
`−β(k₁·k₀)` ≈ −2 undamped (because `gk=0` between a token's factors) and is downcast to bf16 —
always invertible, but ill-conditioned exactly where large β and correlated Householder directions
live. **Day one, verify throughput first, not correctness:** microbenchmark
`chunk_dplr_delta_rule` at `T_dplr = 2·4096`, BT=16, H16/K64/V64 against the current
0.705× fused-recurrent kernel. Because DPLR builds four A-matrices at 2× rows in 16-wide chunks
and recomputes its forward in the backward, it may well be *slower* than the kernel it is meant to
replace — and that single measurement either justifies the week or saves it. Then, before any
training run, check `A_ab_inv` conditioning at β→2 with near-parallel `k₀`,`k₁`, and diff the
wrapper against `kda_householder_torch` with the `a_0 = exp(g)⊙k_0` correction in place.
(Also worth a one-line fix: our own `kda_householder.py:536-537` overstates its workspace as
6.0/48 GiB where the true values are 4.0/32 GiB.)
