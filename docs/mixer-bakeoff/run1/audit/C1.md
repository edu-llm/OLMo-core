## F2 — CONFIRMED — Root cause: a sequential-over-T, non-tensor-core, 3.7%-occupancy kernel. It is **latency/serialization-bound**, secondarily bandwidth-bound. It is NOT FLOP-bound.

`KDA_BASE` calls `fla.ops.kda.chunk_kda` — a **chunked** WY-representation kernel
(`recurrent.py:913`, via `attention/flash_linear_attn_api.py:45`), with
`use_qk_l2norm_in_kernel=True` and `use_gate_in_kernel=True` (both fused).

`KDA_R1/R2` call the in-tree `chunk_kda_householder`
(`src/olmo_core/nn/attention/kda_householder.py`), which despite the `chunk_` prefix is
**sequential over time**. The file says so itself at lines 68-73: *"This is a **simple
fused-recurrent (sequential-over-time) Triton kernel**, *not* a chunked WY-representation kernel
… It is expected to be materially slower than fla's chunked kernels; the goal of this milestone is
mechanism validation, not throughput."* **The deficit was declared in the source before the run.**

Four mechanical defects, in order of impact:

**(a) A T=4096-long serial dependency chain, at 3.7% occupancy.** `grid = (NV, N*H)` = `(8, B*H)`
(`kda_householder.py:264`, `:553`). At B=2, H=16 that is **256 programs** with `num_warps=1`
(`:258`, since `BK=64`), i.e. **256 warps against the A100's 6,912 warp slots = 3.7%
occupancy**, 2.37 waves over 108 SMs. Each program then walks `for _ in range(0, T)` — 4,096
loop-carried iterations in the forward (`:181`) and **two more full T-length loops in the
backward** (`:373` recompute pass, `:414` reverse pass). Measured: 1.782 µs per T-iteration ≈
2,513 cycles at 1.41 GHz, ~1,060 cycles per wave. That is pure dependent-latency exposure — the
loop carries `b_h` in registers, so nothing overlaps, and there are not enough programs to hide
the latency with other work. A chunked kernel shortens this chain by `BT` (64x at BT=64) *and*
converts the inner contraction into tensor-core GEMMs.

**(b) No tensor cores at all.** The state contraction is
`b_u = b_beta * (b_v - tl.sum(b_h * b_k[:, None], 0))` (`:196`) — an elementwise multiply plus a
`tl.sum` reduction, **not** `tl.dot`. Same for the readout `tl.sum(b_h * b_q[:,None], 0)` (`:201`)
and every backward gradient (`:434`, `:456`, `:462`, `:467`). Everything runs in **fp32 on CUDA
cores**: A100 peak 19.5 TFLOP/s versus 312 TFLOP/s bf16 tensor — a **16x** headroom the kernel
cannot touch. Measured achieved rate: **0.69 TFLOP/s fp32 at R=1** (3.5% of fp32 peak,
**0.22% of tensor peak**), 0.80 TFLOP/s at R=2.

**(c) The O(B·T·H·K·V) fp32 workspace — real HBM traffic, ~20% of peak.** See F3.

**(d) `O(R²)` recompute in the backward.** `:440-449` re-walks the R-chain from `b_s0` for each
factor rather than holding R+1 tiles (the comment at `:422-423` says this is deliberate, to avoid
spilling at BK=128). At R=2 that is cheap; it becomes the dominant term at R≥4.

**Not the cause — REFUTED:** it is not FLOP-bound (0.22% of tensor peak, 3.5% of fp32 peak —
there is no arithmetic wall in sight); it is not kernel-launch-bound in the `O(T)`-launches sense
(the T-loop is *inside* one kernel — there are exactly 2 launches per layer per microbatch, one
fwd one bwd, i.e. ~32/step, negligible); and it is not primarily register/spill-bound (BK=64 gives
a `[64,8]` fp32 tile = 2 KB, comfortably in registers, and `num_warps` is already widened at
`:258` to keep it off the spill path).

### The L2 trap check (per the standing lesson)

- **Live state working set:** `[BK=64, BV=8]` fp32 = **2 KB per program**; 256 programs = **512 KB
  total**. This **fits in A100's 40 MB L2** — so the *state* recurrence is a register/L2 resident
  workload and any roofline claim about *it* would be untrustworthy.
- **But the `hs` workspace being streamed is 2.00 GiB — 51x L2.** It cannot be cached, so its
  traffic is genuine HBM traffic. **This is why the bandwidth number below is believable and the
  state-tile number is not.** Stating both, as required.
- **Achieved bandwidth against peak: 107.4 GB per step per GPU in 0.3504 s = 306 GB/s = 19.7%
  of A100-40GB HBM2e's 1555 GB/s.** Real but not saturating — consistent with
  latency-bound-with-substantial-bandwidth-cost, not bandwidth-saturated. A bandwidth-bound
  kernel would sit at 70-90%.

**The largest single tensor, `hs` at 2.00 GiB, is 79% of the 2.54 GiB measured delta. The clue
points at exactly one tensor, and the HANDOFF's identification of it is correct.**

**One correction to the HANDOFF's memory arithmetic:** `HANDOFF.md:238` quotes

bf16), not 32 MiB. The **128x ratio** the argument rests on is unchanged and correct; both
absolute numbers are stated at the wrong batch size.

### PRODUCTION CONSEQUENCE — the penalty scales with the linear-attention ratio

Per-layer overhead, measured: **175.2 ms/layer (R=1)** and **267.1 ms/layer (R=2)**, over
KDA_BASE's 1.2351 s step. Extrapolating linearly (each additional Householder layer displaces a
mixer whose own cost is small against 175-267 ms, so the overhead is additive):

| Householder layers | R=1 step | R=1 tok/s ratio | R=2 step | R=2 tok/s ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2 (measured)** | 1.5855 s | **0.779x** | 1.7692 s | **0.705x** |
| 4 | 1.936 s | 0.638x | 2.303 s | 0.536x |
| 8 | 2.637 s | 0.468x | 3.372 s | 0.366x |
| 14 | 3.688 s | 0.335x | 4.974 s | 0.248x |
| 16 | 4.038 s | 0.306x | 5.508 s | 0.224x |

**This inverts the reassuring reading of the run.** The 0.779x/0.705x figures are properties of a

whole-step penalty converges on the mixer-local factor as the ratio rises.** Any decision that
reads "22%" as the production cost of R>1 is understating it by up to an order of magnitude.

### Memory scaling — the *good* news, and the real cliff

**The reserved-memory penalty does NOT scale with layer count.** `hs` is allocated inside
`kda_householder_bwd` and freed when that layer's backward returns (`:539` — a plain local
tensor, no `ctx.save_for_backward`), so only one layer's workspace is live at a time. Reserved
would stay ~flat at +2.5-2.7 GiB regardless of whether 2 or 14 layers carry the operator. **So
memory does not block a production ratio; time does.**

**But it scales linearly in B·T, and that is the cliff:**

| | B=1 | B=2 |
|---|---|---|
| T=4,096 | 1.00 GiB | **2.00 GiB (this run)** |
| T=8,192 | 2.00 | 4.00 |
| T=16,384 | 4.00 | 8.00 |
| T=32,768 | 8.00 | 16.00 |

At K=V=128 (a plausible production head dim) multiply by 4: **32 GiB at B=1/T=8,192**, and the
`:534` docstring's "48 GiB, will OOM a single GPU" case is reached at B8/T4096/K128/V128.
**R>1 at production context is not memory-feasible without chunking, independent of the speed
argument.** This is the stronger half of the case for the rewrite and the HANDOFF is right to
lead with it.

## F6 — SUSPECTED — The `sd = 0.04516` outlier on KDA_R1 has a mechanical candidate in this kernel, and the HANDOFF's proposed check is the right one.

`KDA_R1`'s seed sd is 0.04516 — 17x `KDA_NOACT`'s — on *mathematically identical* computation.
The kernel accumulates the recurrent state in fp32 **sequentially over 4,096 steps** (`:186-197`),
where `chunk_kda` accumulates over ~64 chunk boundaries with tensor-core-blocked reductions. A
4,096-deep serial fp32 accumulation has a materially longer error chain. Additionally the
backward's `.sum(0)` over the NV axis (`:585-588`) is a plain reduction (deliberately, for
bit-reproducibility — `:507-508`), but pass 2 *re-derives* `S^(0)` from a stored pre-decay state
multiplied by `exp(g)` (`:419`), which is not the same rounding as the forward's path. **This is
a plausible mechanism, not a demonstrated one — n=3 with one outlier cannot separate it from
seed noise.** `HANDOFF.md:246`'s proposed check (`backend="torch"`, the bit-exact reference at
`kda_householder_torch.py`) is the right instrument and is cheap.

Note also `RESULTS-TABLE.html:265` says R1's sd is "**4x** any other arm" while
`HANDOFF.md:243` and `RESULTS-TABLE.html:152` say "**17x**" — an internal inconsistency in the
same deliverable. 0.04516/0.00258 = 17.5x (vs KDA_NOACT, the smallest); 0.04516/0.01678 = 2.7x
(vs KDA_GCONV, the largest other). Neither document says which comparator it used.

# Deliverables

## (a) The corrected MFU picture

**Strike MFU from the deliverable. Do not correct it — retract it.**

1. `mfu_pct` is **not comparable across arms**, because its numerator (`flops_per_token`) is a
   per-arm quantity computed under **two different conventions** (6 FLOP/param for
   FFN/LM-head/Attention/**GDN2**, 2 FLOP/param for **KDA/Householder/LIV**), summed without
   normalisation.
2. **GDN2's "highest MFU measured, 42.0%" is an artifact.** Its mixer is the only one counted at
   6/param, giving it a 1.58% larger numerator. It actually delivers **0.9943x KDA_BASE's
   throughput** — it is *slower*. Correcting the convention drops it to third
   (44.21 vs KDA_BASE's 44.44), matching the throughput ranking exactly. **`HANDOFF.md:255` uses
   this artifact to soften the GDN2 quality verdict and must be struck.**
3. **The R arms' MFU is understated for the opposite reason.** `solve_widths` shrinks 14 FFN
   layers (which count at 6/param) to pay for a bigger mixer (counted at 2/param), so KDA_R2's
   counted total *falls* 0.66% as its real work rises. Corrected MFU: R1 32.35 → 34.61,
   R2 29.11 → 31.36.
4. **Once all six arms use one convention, MFU carries no information beyond throughput** — the
   arms are FLOP-matched to within 0.04%, so corrected MFU is throughput times a constant. That
   is the argument for deleting the column rather than fixing it.
5. **Nothing that matters depends on it.** Throughput is measured wall-clock and never touches
   `flops_per_token`. `REPORT.md` — the generated, pre-registered artifact carrying the actual

   memory result, and the CE null all survive intact.** The bug is confined to hand-written prose.
   Six specific strings need striking (listed in F1 §2).

## (b) Mechanical root-cause verdict, with the arithmetic

**The Householder kernel is latency/serialization-bound on a T-length serial chain at 3.7%
occupancy, executing entirely on fp32 CUDA cores with zero tensor-core use, while streaming a
2 GiB fp32 workspace it did not need to materialise. It is not FLOP-bound and not
launch-count-bound.**

| evidence | number | source |
|---|---|---|
| Serial chain length | 4,096 loop-carried iterations, **×3 loops** (1 fwd + 2 bwd) | `kda_householder.py:181`, `:373`, `:414` |
| Occupancy | 256 programs × 1 warp / 6,912 slots = **3.7%**; 2.37 waves | `:264`, `:553`, `:258` |
| Per-iteration latency | 1.782 µs ≈ **2,513 cycles** @1.41 GHz | derived from 0.3504 s / 12,288 iters / 16 invocations |
| Tensor cores used | **none** — `tl.sum(b_h*b_k[:,None],0)`, no `tl.dot` | `:196`, `:201`, `:434`, `:456`, `:462` |
| Achieved arithmetic | **0.69 TFLOP/s fp32** = 3.5% of 19.5 peak = **0.22% of 312 bf16 peak** | 0.241 TFLOP/step ÷ 0.3504 s |
| Achieved bandwidth | 107.4 GB/step ÷ 0.3504 s = **306 GB/s = 19.7% of 1555 GB/s** | `hs` 2×2.0 GiB + partials, ×8 microbatch ×2 layers |
| **Working set (L2 check)** | state tile 512 KB total → **fits 40 MB L2, untrustworthy**; `hs` 2.00 GiB → **51× L2, real HBM** | `BK=64,BV=8` fp32; `:539` |
| Reserved-memory prediction | **2.770 / 3.039 GiB** vs measured **2.539 / 2.670** (8%/12%) | `:539-548` at B2/T4096/H16/K64/V64/NV8 |
| Mixer FLOP share (corrected) | **2.154%** (counted 0.768%) | reproduced from source |
| **Mixer-local factor** | **R=1: 5.4x-14.2x · R=2: 7.7x-21.1x** | `(1−s)b + s·b·f = r`, s ∈ [2.2%, 6.5%] |
| Overhead as share of step | **22.1% (R1) / 30.2% (R2)** from 2 of 16 layers | 0.3504/1.5855, 0.5341/1.7692 |
| Ceiling if fully fixed | **+28.4% (R1) / +43.2% (R2)** throughput | removing the whole delta |
| **At a production LA ratio** | **8-of-16: 0.468x / 0.366x · 14-of-16: 0.335x / 0.248x** | 175.2 / 267.1 ms per layer |

**Is it fixable? YES, and the diagnosis says the proposed fix is the right one.** Chunking to
`chunk_dplr_delta_rule` attacks all four defects simultaneously: it shortens the serial chain
**64x** (T=4096 → 64 chunk steps), replaces the elementwise-plus-reduce contraction with
tensor-core GEMMs (**16x** peak headroom), raises occupancy by giving the grid a chunk axis, and
eliminates `hs` entirely — **2.00 GiB → 16.0 MiB, a 128x reduction**. The ceiling on the speed
half is **+28.4% at R=1 / +43.2% at R=2** whole-step *at the 2-of-16 placement*, and materially
more at a higher ratio.

**But the honest investment framing is the memory case, not the speed case**, and for a reason the
HANDOFF gets right: this run found **no CE gain** waiting behind the overhead (MDE 0.0636 nats,
nothing clears it). So the rewrite buys *feasibility*, not a win — and it should be funded only
if someone intends to test R>1 at production context, where the fp32 workspace is a hard OOM
(32 GiB at B1/T8192/K128/V128) rather than a 20% tax. **Two independent conclusions from this
diagnosis stand regardless of the rewrite: (i) never ship `KimiDeltaHouseholder` at R=1 — it is
the same function 28.4% slower, and (ii) do not read 0.779x/0.705x as portable — at a production
linear-attention ratio they become 0.31x-0.47x / 0.22x-0.37x.**

## (c) The single cheapest confirming measurement, and where it must run

**A per-kernel timing breakdown of one training step under `torch.profiler`, comparing `KDA_BASE`
against `KDA_R1` at the exact run geometry — reporting, per CUDA kernel: name, count, total
device time, and achieved DRAM read+write bytes.**

**Precisely what it must show to confirm the diagnosis** (all four, or the diagnosis is wrong):
1. `kda_householder_fwd_kernel` + `kda_householder_bwd_kernel` account for **≥95% of the
   0.3504 s/step delta**, at **exactly 2 launches per layer per microbatch** (~32/step, not
   O(T)) — this is what separates *serialization inside a kernel* from *launch overhead*, and it
   is the distinction that decides whether chunking helps.
2. The kernel names contain **no `mma`/`hmma`/tensor-core-bearing variants**, and Nsight's
   `sm__inst_executed_pipe_tensor` is **~0** — confirming the 16x headroom is untouched.

   confirming latency-bound rather than bandwidth-saturated.
4. A `torch.cuda.memory_reserved()` read **immediately before and after** one Householder
   backward shows a step of **≈2.77 GiB (R=1) / 3.04 GiB (R=2)**, with
