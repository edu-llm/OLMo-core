# D1 — What more GPU money buys on the mixer bake-off, and whether it is worth it

**Bottom line up front:** Yes there is a winner (`KDA_BASE` / `KDA_NOACT`, tied), and no, more
seeds are not worth it. Resolving CE to the literature gap costs **2–7x the production run it
would inform**. Spend **$79–171 on a sliced/recall re-eval of checkpoints you already own**, then
ship. Total recommended spend: **~$150. Do not spend $6,258 on n=95.**

## 0. Verification of the inputs

Every number in the brief reproduces exactly from `docs/mixer-bakeoff/run1/cell-*.json` (18 cells).
Independently recomputed from raw cells:

| quantity | my recompute | published | match |
|---|---|---|---|

| chi-square bracket | [0.01464, 0.03370] | [0.01464, 0.03370] | exact |

**Estimator, stated once and used everywhere:** MDE = lambda\* x sigma x sqrt(2/n), where
lambda\* is the noncentrality solving `P(T > c_Dunnett) = 0.80` for **exact noncentral t
(dominant-tail survival only)**, integrated by 40-point Gauss–Legendre over the chi density of
`S = sqrt(chi2_df/df)` in 6 panels. Dunnett `c` from the equicorrelated max-|t| integral
(rho=0.5). Three independent validations of my own implementation:

1. vs `scipy.stats.nct.sf` / `t.sf` — agreement to **12 decimal places** on 8 test points;
2. Dunnett at k=1 reduces to the Student t 0.975 quantile — **2.178812830 vs 2.178812830** (9 dp);
3. reproduces the run's published 2.9013 and 0.06360.

I report the normal approximation only to show the gap. At n=3 it gives 0.06239 vs the exact
0.06360 — **1.9% optimistic**, shrinking to 0.2% by n=20. (Note: this is the *MDE-at-fixed-power*
gap, which is small. The 2.2x error recorded in project memory was on a different quantity —
required-n / power at fixed effect — where the same approximation error is amplified through the
inverse. Both directions of that trap are avoided here by never leaving exact nct.)

## 3. Deeper (more tokens) instead of more seeds

Let per-cell effect scale as `E(B) ~ B^-p` and per-cell sigma as `sigma(B) ~ B^-q`. Resolvability
is `E/MDE ~ B^(q-p)`, so **doubling tokens multiplies resolvability by 2^(q-p)** — an improvement
**iff q > p**.

**p is measured in-tree:** GDN's edge 0.0103 @1B -> 0.0059 @15B gives
`p = ln(0.0103/0.0059)/ln(15) = **0.2058**`. Each doubling shrinks the effect to 2^-0.2058 =
**0.8671x**.

**q is NOT measured — the run has exactly one token budget, so sigma(B) is unidentified in-tree.**
That is the single biggest hole in any "just train longer" proposal. Bracketing:

| assumption about sigma | q | resolvability per doubling | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| sigma budget-independent | 0.00 | **x0.8671** | **strictly WORSE** |
| **break-even** | **0.2058** | x1.0000 | neutral |
| sigma ~ 1/sqrt(tokens) | 0.50 | x1.2262 | better |

> **BREAK-EVEN CONDITION, explicitly: sigma must fall at least as fast as B^-0.2058 — i.e. drop by
> >= 13.29% per doubling of tokens — for a longer run to be no worse than the current one. Cost
> rises 100% per doubling.**

Even the *favourable* q=0.5 case buys only 1.23x resolvability for 2x the money — worse
value-per-dollar than seeds, which buy sqrt(n) for linear cost. And the trap in the brief is real:
**at q=0 a bigger run is strictly worse on both axes simultaneously** — less resolvable *and* more
expensive. Cost of a 15x-token repeat of the current design: **$2,964 (brief) / $5,336 (true)** for
an effect shrunk to 0.573x.

**SUSPECTED (mechanism, not measured): q is likely closer to 0 than to 0.5 for this endpoint.**
Seed variance here is dominated by init-lottery divergence in *which minimum is reached*, not by
Monte-Carlo error in the eval average — the eval set is fixed at 975M tokens for every cell, so the
measurement noise on `val_ce` is negligible and cannot shrink with training length. Under that
reading, **deeper runs are the worst of the four options.** Cheap way to settle it: the retained
**step-572 checkpoints** give a free second budget point (0.3B vs 0.6B tokens) — see §5.

**Verdict: UNSUPPORTED as an investment. Do not buy tokens to resolve CE.**

   demonstrate. It should be **pre-registered before the next run**, not asserted after.

**Verdict: the best option *if* you insist on resolving CE — but §6 shows you should not insist.**

## 5. A different endpoint — best value-per-dollar available

`sliced_eval` is `null` in **all 18 cells**; `--slice-mask-uri` was never passed. Because
checkpoints were retained, this is a **re-weighted forward pass over held-out text with zero
retraining**.

| scope | eval time | node-h | **cost** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 surviving arms x n=3 = 12 cells | 18 min | 3.60 | **$79.05** |
| 12 cells, worst-case contention | 26 min | 5.20 | **$114.18** |
| all 18 cells | 18 min | 5.40 | **$118.57** |
| all 18 cells, worst case | 26 min | 7.80 | **$171.27** |

**Why it can resolve what CE cannot.** Hymba reports **-20.75 recall points at near-identical
perplexity** — direct evidence that mixer differences live somewhere aggregate CE integrates away.
Aggregate CE over 975M tokens averages recall-critical positions (long-range retrieval, rare
continuations) together with the overwhelming mass of locally-predictable tokens, where all six
arms are identical by construction (same 6 global-attention layers at 2,5,8,10,12,14). A sliced
endpoint changes the *estimand*, not just the precision.

Required effect/sigma for resolution, 4-arm design (this is the honest bar):

| n | MDE in sigma units |
|---|---|
| 3 | 3.119 |
| 5 | 2.198 |
| 8 | 1.674 |

For calibration, the CE endpoint sits at d = 0.49 (0.010 nats) to 1.47 (0.030 nats) — i.e. CE is
underpowered because its **effect-to-noise ratio is below 3.1**, not because nats are small.
A recall endpoint wins iff its d exceeds ~3.1 at n=3. Hymba's 20.75-point gap is plausibly far
above that, **but I cannot verify the ratio without knowing seed sigma on the sliced metric —
which is exactly what $79 buys.**

**Two engineering caveats that the "zero cost" framing hides, and that I must price honestly:**

- **`build_slice_masks.py` DOES NOT EXIST.** It is referenced in three docstrings

  what defines a recall-critical position.
- **`evaluate_sliced` is rank-gated and will HANG under FSDP.** Line 2098 is
  `if get_rank() == 0:` — the same defect the code's own comment (2088-2094) documents as
  survivable-but-not-correct, mitigated only by a barrier. This must be fixed to run on every rank

So the true price is **~$79–171 of GPU plus 2–4 engineer-days**, not literally zero. Even so it is

**Free bonus from the same checkpoints:** the retained **step-572** snapshots give a second token

## 7. Seed structure — does pairing lower the effective sigma?

Data seeds **are** shared across arms (210007/220014/230021) and the run.yaml confirms the intent:
data order is a function of `(data_seed + epoch, len(dataset))`, independent of the model, with
sequence length / batch / world size as literals. Init seeds are unique and explicitly **not**
paired ("arms have different tensor inventories, so a shared init stream diverges at the first
differing tensor"). So a randomized-complete-block analysis on data seed is legitimate. I computed
it.

**Two-way ANOVA (arm + data_seed), 6 arms x 3 blocks:**

| source | SS | df | |
|---|---|---|---|
| arm | 5.187374e-03 | 5 | |
| **data_seed (block)** | **9.070656e-04** | **2** | F(2,10) = **1.1077**, p = **0.3677** |
| residual (blocked) | 4.094259e-03 | 10 | sigma = **0.020234** |
| within (unblocked) | 5.001325e-03 | 12 | sigma = **0.020415** |

**CONFIRMED: blocking does not help — it slightly hurts.** sigma falls only
0.020415 -> 0.020234 (**0.9%**), while df falls 12 -> 10. Net effect on the MDE, exact nct
throughout:

| n | unblocked MDE | blocked MDE | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 0.06360 (df 12, c=2.9013) | **0.06481** (df 10, c=2.9903) | **1.019 — worse** |
| 5 | 0.04609 | 0.04628 | 1.004 — worse |
| 8 | 0.03545 | 0.03539 | 0.998 — break-even |

**The 2 df you spend on the block cost more than the 0.9% variance it removes, until roughly
n=8, where it becomes a wash. No number in any table above changes.** The seed-mean spread
(3.05246 / 3.06931 / 3.05719) is almost entirely one cell: `KDA_R1` at seed 220014 = 3.12675.
Re-running the block test **without R1** gives F(2,8) = 0.8444, p = 0.4648 and sigma 0.009605
unblocked vs 0.009758 blocked — blocking is *still* not helping.

**Mechanistic reading: the shared data seed is not a real block because it does not induce a
common shift.** The dominant noise source is the init lottery (unpaired by design), not data
order — which is consistent with the §3 claim that sigma will not shrink with token budget either.
The data-seed sharing is still valuable for **reproducibility**, just not for **power**.

### Process finding (as requested): what else in the deliverable was never cross-checked?

The MFU defect shipped in a final table because it was never compared against a second,
independent measurement. Auditing the deliverable for the same pattern — a reported column with no
independent cross-check:

- **`mfu_pct` — BROKEN, confirmed.** Only derivable quantity in the report with no second source.
  It had one available and unused: `throughput x flops_per_token` is an identity that would have
  exposed it immediately (as it did for me in one line).
- **`flops_per_token` — the root cause, and it is reported raw in every cell JSON.** Cross-checkable
  against an analytic `6ND` estimate; never was. Note `KDA_R2` reports **fewer** FLOPs/token than
  `KDA_BASE` (2,457,037,312 vs 2,473,388,544) despite a strictly larger operator — visibly
  impossible, and sitting in the artifact unflagged.
- **`peak_memory_gib` — cross-checked, sound.** sd exactly 0.000 within every arm across 3 seeds is
  itself a strong internal check, and the handoff records an independent prediction matching to
  three decimals. **But note `peak_memory_reserved_gib` is where the R-arm penalty actually lives
  (13.26/13.39 vs 10.72), and `peak_memory_gib` shows only 1.007x/1.023x** — reporting peak alone
  understates the R arms' cost by ~24%. Both are in the JSON; the report's §3.1 table shows only
  peak.
- **`throughput_tok_s_steady` — cross-checked, sound.** The report verifies `tps_device_avg` sits
  below it on all 18 cells, and median/p90 step times move consistently (R2 at 1.432x/1.430x
  matches 0.705x throughput to within 1%). Two independent instruments agree. **This is why my
  recommendation rests on it.**
- **`val_ce` — cross-checked, sound.** `val_tokens_present` asserted exactly against the manifest,
  identical 974,917,632 scored tokens on all 18 cells.

**Pattern: the metrics that were cross-checked are sound; the one that was not is wrong.** The
FLOP counter had a free identity check available and unused. Recommend: assert
`mfu = throughput x flops_per_token / device_peak` and a consistency check that a strictly larger
operator cannot report fewer FLOPs, before any future report prints MFU.
