## FINDING 1 — REFUTED (most serious). The GDN2 "significant" result is NOT significant. The quoted T3 interval has no multiplicity correction, and the correctly-corrected one spans zero.

This is the finding that should change the spending decision.

`HANDOFF.md:55-56` and `RESULTS-TABLE.html` §3:

> Under the pre-registered unequal-variance fallback (Welch F = 53.29, p = 0.0005) GDN2's
> Dunnett-T3 CI is **[+0.0262, +0.0479], excluding zero**.

and `RESULTS-TABLE.html` §1b, GDN2 cons:

> The **only** arm whose contrast is significant under any test (Welch/T3 CI [+0.0262, +0.0479],
> p = 0.0005), so this is the least likely result to be noise.

I ran the analysis script's **own** `welch_t3_contrasts()` (`scripts/analyse_bakeoff.py:975`) on the
real 18 cells:

| arm | est | Welch se | welch_df | SMM crit | T3 95% CI | excludes_zero |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GDN2 | +0.037055 | 0.006631 | 2.5548 | **5.7087** | **[−0.000801, +0.074911]** | **False** |

Reverse-engineering the written interval: half-width `(0.0479−0.0262)/2 = 0.010850`, divided by the

studentized-maximum-modulus critical value the text explicitly names is **5.7087** at k=5,
df=2.5548, which is 3.5× larger.

The centre of the written CI (+0.037050) matches the true estimate (+0.037055), so the estimate is
right and only the interval is wrong.

**Consequence:** the claim "the only contrast significant under any test" is **false**. Under the
pooled analysis nothing is significant (`REPORT.md:64`, correctly). Under the pre-registered
unequal-variance fallback *as implemented in this repo's own script*, nothing is significant
either. `HANDOFF.md:56` ("Both readings are in the report; neither is presented as settled") and
`HANDOFF.md:249-256` (⓸ "Handle the GDN2 result carefully with Tom Liu … the only contrast

Note also `REPORT.md` never contains this interval — `results.json` has `welch_fallback: null`, and

## FINDING 3 — SUSPECTED (severe, forensic). 16 of the 18 cell files fail bit-exact identities that `summarise()` guarantees. Their throughput/timing/loss fields were not produced by the code as written.

The entrypoint contains **zero** `round()` calls (verified: `grep -n "round(" .edullm/train_core6_arm.py` → no hits). So every float it emits should be a full-precision float64.

**Test A — the airtight one.** `.edullm/train_core6_arm.py:1755` computes
`steady_per_device = steady_total / world_size` with `world_size = 8`. Division by a power of two is
**exact** in IEEE-754, so `per_device * 8 == total` must hold *bit-for-bit* in all 18 cells.

It holds in **5 of 18**. Violated in cells **1, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17** — up to
**111 tok/s** of discrepancy (cell-10: `52,769 × 8 = 422,152` vs reported `422,041`). The same test
on `whole_run_per_device` fails in **16 of 18**.

**Test B.** `throughput_tok_s_all_steps` (our arithmetic, line 1741) and `tps_total_avg` (upstream
`SpeedMonitorCallback`, line 1946 — a different code path over a different window) are
**bit-identical in 15 of 18 cells**. Two independent measurements cannot agree to the last bit.
They differ, correctly, in exactly cells **0, 1, 2**.

**Test C.** `throughput_tok_s_steady == tokens_in_steady_window / steady_window_seconds` is an
internal identity. Exact in **2 of 18** (cells 0 and 2); elsewhere off by up to 0.026%.

**Test D — precision census.** Count of low-precision (≤11 significant digit) float fields per cell,
out of 18 float fields:

| cells | # short fields |
|---|---|
| 0, 2 | 3 |
| 1 | 8 |
| 3, 15 | 12 |
| 9 | 13 |
| 4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 16 | 14 |
| 5, 14, 17 | 15 |

**Test E.** `first_loss` is *exactly* `11.7124` in **16 of 18** cells — a 4-decimal value that is
**not** float32-representable (`float32(11.7124) = 11.712400436401367`), so it cannot be a rounded
`train/CE loss` tensor read. Cells 0 and 2 carry genuine float32 values
(`11.712315559387207`, `11.7125825881958`). `seconds` is an exact integer in 17 of 18;
`throughput_tok_s_steady` is an exact integer in 13 of 18; `steady_window_seconds` is an exact
integer in 9.

**Interpretation.** Cells **0 and 2** look like authentic `summarise()` output. The other 16 look
transcribed at reduced precision and then re-derived inconsistently — which is consistent with
`HANDOFF.md:153-155` (⑥ "The summary JSON is not in S3 … Results come from **CloudWatch**"): the
numbers were read back out of log text rather than copied as bytes.

**What this does and does not damage.** Crucially, `val_ce` (16–17 significant digits in all 18) and
`peak_memory_gib` / `peak_memory_reserved_gib` (full precision, and exact MiB multiples) are
**untouched**. So the CE analysis and the memory analysis rest on genuine data. The damage is to
throughput/timing/`first_loss`/`last_loss`. Because the throughput *effects* are large (0.705×,
0.779×) and ±111 tok/s is ~0.03%, the throughput **ranking** survives — but no throughput digit
past the third is trustworthy, and the "±0.05%"-style precision claims are not supportable.

I cannot prove intent from the artifacts alone, hence SUSPECTED. **The 16 cells' provenance needs
to be re-derived from CloudWatch before any of these numbers is quoted again.**
